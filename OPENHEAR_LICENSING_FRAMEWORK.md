# OpenHear Licensing Framework

**UK Certification Mark UK00004343685**  
**Purpose:** keep OpenHear certificates free for recipients while licensing the
right to assess, issue, and commercially support certification under controlled
quality rules.

---

## 1. Core position

OpenHear certificates remain free to display for valid recipients.

Revenue comes from licensing the **right to issue certificates** to approved
auditors, consultancies, accessibility specialists, healthcare bodies, standards
partners, and other controlled issuers. The mark is not sold as a badge. It is a
quality-controlled certification signal tied to real individual human review.

The governing question remains:

> **"Was a human member of the team able to personally review the specific facts
> of my specific situation?"**

## 4. Competitive position

OpenHear does not compete by becoming a slower, larger standards body. It
competes by being narrow, clear, and enforceable. The certificate answers one
specific accountability question: whether a real human can personally review the
specific facts before a process affects an identified individual.

Traditional standards bodies can provide broad institutional assurance. OpenHear
provides a sharper trust signal for accessibility, complaints, public services,
automated decision processes, and disability-related scrutiny. Internal standards
cannot provide the same independent mark governance because they are self-claims.

The defensible advantage is the split between open materials and controlled mark
use. The repository can remain open under MIT terms. The official certification
mark remains controlled, traceable, and enforceable.

## 5. Early adopter protection

Existing public-interest recipients are protected. Certificates already issued
remain free to display under their original terms and should not be converted
into paid subscriptions.

The public explanation is simple: **recipients do not pay to display a valid
OpenHear certificate. Commercial partners pay only if they want the right to
assess, issue, integrate, or commercially package certification services.**

Councils and public services should be treated as public-interest proof, not as
a revenue source. Optional support, training, or implementation work may be
offered separately, but the certificate-display right remains free for valid
recipients.

## 6. Quality control

Approved issuers must:

1. follow the published assessment process;
2. retain evidence for each certificate issued;
3. avoid vague or inflated compliance claims;
4. distinguish clearly between assessment, support, and certification;
5. never sell mark display separately from a valid assessment;
6. record SOVEREIGN / NULL / AMBIGUOUS outcomes accurately; and
7. escalate NULL or AMBIGUOUS outcomes for individual human review.

The mark may not be used to badge unchanged automated decision pipelines.

## 7. Objection handling

**"Why should we pay?"**  
You pay only if you want to issue certificates, package assessment services, or
represent yourself as an approved OpenHear partner. Recipients do not pay merely
to display a valid certificate.

**"BSI is bigger. Why not use them?"**  
BSI and similar bodies are broad standards institutions. OpenHear is a focused
certification mark for meaningful individual human scrutiny. A large standards
body may become a partner, but it cannot replace the controlled mark.

**"Is this profiting from accessibility?"**  
The model is designed to avoid charging public-interest recipients. Commercial
partners fund verification, quality control, training, and mark enforcement.

**"Can this stay free?"**  
Certificate display can stay free for recipients. Issuer governance cannot be
free at scale because the register, training, misuse enforcement, and quality
control need sustainable resources.

**"Can we self-certify?"**  
Organisations may use the open materials internally under the MIT licence. They
may not present themselves as OpenHear certified, Burgess certified, or approved
to issue certificates without permission.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

- **Pay-to-play perception:** keep display free for recipients and charge only
  issuers or commercial partners.
- **Rubber-stamping:** require evidence retention, random quality review,
  suspension rights, and public delisting.
- **Mark dilution:** keep a public register, clear scope statements, annual
  partner renewal, and correction notices for misuse.
- **Early adopter resentment:** grandfather existing certificates and describe
  public-sector recipients as public-interest adopters, not future subscribers.
- **Overclaim risk:** state clearly that OpenHear does not replace law, WCAG,
  safety duties, accessibility duties, or sector regulation.
- **Anti-monetisation conflict:** prohibit "Burgess-compliant" product badging
  where automated decision systems remain unchanged.
