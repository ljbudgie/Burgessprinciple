## The framework in one sentence

The Burgess Principle is a binary test applied to any exercise of institutional power over an individual: *Was a named human being's mind applied to the specific facts of this person's case before institutional power was exercised?*

---

## The three outcomes

| Answer | Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Yes — named human, specific facts, before the decision | **SOVEREIGN** | The process was lawful and individually accountable |
| No — automated, batch, or status-based processing | **NULL** | No individual human review took place |
| Vague process language without individual confirmation | **AMBIGUOUS** | The institution has not confirmed compliance — escalate |

---

## Why NULL has legal weight

A NULL finding is not a moral critique. It has a documented legal consequence.

**Statute:** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, s.80 (in force 5 February 2026) requires *meaningful human involvement* in automated decisions that significantly affect individuals. A NULL decision — one made without that involvement — fails the statutory threshold. The affected person has a statutory right to make representations, to named human intervention with authority to change the outcome, and to contest the decision.

**Administrative law:** Where an act of institutional power requires individual human review and that review did not occur, the act may be **void ab initio** — without legal effect from the moment it was made. *HM Treasury v Ahmed (No. 2)* [2010] UKSC 5 confirms that acts taken without required authority are void, not merely voidable. *R (Majera) v SSHD* [2021] UKSC 46 adds nuance: the consequence matters more than the label — an unlawful act cannot found enforceable rights against the individual regardless of whether it is formally characterised as void.

**In practice:** A debt demand, a warrant, an enforcement instruction, or a credit entry founded on a NULL process has no lawful basis for the downstream action it purports to authorise.

---

## What a NULL finding means in practice

- The institution has no lawfully documented basis for the decision.
- Any enforcement action founded on it is challengeable.
- The person retains the right to a named human reviewer with authority to change the outcome.
- Where the person has a protected characteristic, the institution may be in breach of EA 2010 ss.20/21 (anticipatory reasonable adjustment) and s.149 PSED (public authorities only) — regardless of the outcome of any individual complaint.
