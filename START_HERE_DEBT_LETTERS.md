# Debt & Enforcement Letters — First-Time Starter

**If you're stressed and saying “I’m invoking the Burgess Principle as I need
help,” this page is for you.**

The Burgess Principle is the binary test for meaningful human involvement in
automated systems. One question:

> **"Was a human member of the team able to personally review the specific facts of my specific situation?"**

Use that question on your debt or enforcement letter process.

## Step 1 — Send the binary question

Send this by email or post and keep proof of sending:

> Please confirm whether a named human member of your team was able to
> personally review the specific facts of my specific situation before this
> debt/enforcement decision or action.  
>  
> If yes, provide: (1) reviewer name and role, (2) the specific facts reviewed,
> (3) review date, and (4) confirmation this occurred before action.  
>  
> If no individual human review took place, please state that clearly.

## Step 2 — Classify the reply

- **SOVEREIGN** — they name a human reviewer, role, specific facts reviewed, and
  confirm review happened before action.
- **NULL** — they confirm no individual human review occurred; the decision was
  processed, not considered.
- **AMBIGUOUS** — they use vague process language (“human oversight,” “reviewed
  in line with policy”) without naming a reviewer and specific reviewed facts.

**NULL or AMBIGUOUS is the documented starting point for escalation and repair,
not a final verdict.**
