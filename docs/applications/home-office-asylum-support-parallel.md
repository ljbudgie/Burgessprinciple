## 1. What this document establishes

In March 2026, the Home Secretary converted UK asylum support — administered under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 — from a blanket statutory duty into a conditional, evidence-based power. The conversion explicitly requires a named caseworker to review the specific facts of each individual case before the power to withhold or withdraw support is exercised.

That is the Burgess Principle in statute. Not as a campaigning argument. As enacted government policy.

This document maps the parallel precisely, provides the Hansard anchors, and translates the precedent into reusable language for disputes with energy companies, local authorities, medical device providers, banks, AI systems, and any institution that relies on automated blanket processing to avoid named human accountability.

## 2. The policy change: what Parliament enacted

On **5 March 2026**, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced the Migration Reform package to Parliament. The central accountability change converted the asylum support duty under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 from a near-automatic obligation into a conditional power, exercisable only where an individual satisfies specific conduct requirements verified through named caseworker review.

> **"We will remove the current legal obligation to provide asylum support… and restore the power to offer support."**
>
> — Shabana Mahmood, Home Secretary, HC Deb 5 March 2026, col. [Migration Reform statement]

> **"The generosity of the British people will become conditional… Those who require it and play by the rules will rightly continue to receive asylum support. But those who do not will have their support removed."**
>
> — Home Secretary's statement, HC Deb 5 March 2026

**The legal mechanism:** a Statutory Instrument under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999, converting Sections 4, 95, and 98 support from a **duty** (automatic; status-based; no named review required) to a **power** (conditional; individual; named caseworker review required before the power is exercised). Effective March/June 2026.

**The legal significance is precise:** a duty requires an institution to act on status alone — no individual mind need be applied. A power requires an institution to form a judgment about a specific person based on specific facts. The first is architecturally **NULL**. The second, properly exercised, is **SOVEREIGN**.

## 3. The binary parallel — SOVEREIGN vs NULL

| Dimension | Blanket Duty (pre-reform) | Conditional Power (post-reform) |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis for decision** | Immigration status — automatic | Named caseworker's review of individual conduct |
| **Evidence of specific facts** | Not required | Required — evidence of illegal working |
| **Named decision-maker** | None — algorithmic / administrative | Named Home Office caseworker |
| **Audit trail** | System log | Individual decision record, open to judicial review |
| **Recourse if wrong** | General complaint | Judicial review of named officer's specific decision |
| **Burgess classification** | **NULL** | **SOVEREIGN** |

The old system processed support by status. No named human needed to know anything specific about the individual — the duty attached automatically to the classification. That is the definition of **NULL**: institutional power exercised without an individual mind applied to the individual case.

The new system requires a named caseworker to review specific evidence of specific conduct before the power to withhold is exercised. That is the definition of **SOVEREIGN**: a named human applied their mind to the specific facts of the specific case before institutional power was exercised.

**The Home Office did not invent this distinction. It recognised it — and enacted it in law.**

## 4. Why this validates the Burgess Principle's central argument

The Burgess Principle has argued since registration that:

1. Blanket processing of individuals as a class, without individual review, is a structural deficiency — not an administrative inconvenience.
2. The remedy is not more oversight layers or complaint buttons. It is a named human, with authority, reviewing the specific facts of the specific person before institutional power is exercised.
3. That standard is achievable. The same institutions that insist it is impractical already apply it in high-stakes contexts.

The March 2026 Home Office reform is Parliament stating, in statutory form:

- Blanket automatic processing is insufficient when individual conduct is relevant to entitlement.
- A named human must apply their mind to the specific facts before the power is exercised.
- An audit trail of that individual decision must exist and withstand judicial review.

If that standard is appropriate for asylum support — one of the most operationally complex, high-volume areas of government administration — it is appropriate for an energy warrant, a PCN, a benefits sanction, a credit decision, and a medical device fitting.

The Home Office has confirmed the bar. Apply it here.

## 5. The Sections 4 / 95 / 98 mechanism — a legal note

**Section 95, Immigration and Asylum Act 1999** — the primary support provision for destitute asylum seekers. Pre-reform: near-automatic duty once eligibility criteria were met. Post-reform: discretionary power, conditioned on conduct compliance, including no evidence of illegal working.

**Section 4** — support for failed asylum seekers in prescribed circumstances. Same conversion: duty to power.

**Section 98** — temporary support pending Section 95 determination. Same conversion: duty to power.

The Statutory Instrument effecting the change makes conditionality explicit: the decision to withhold or withdraw must be preceded by a named caseworker's assessment of the individual's conduct against specific evidentiary criteria. The decision record must withstand judicial review — meaning it must be individual, reasoned, and attributable to a named officer.

This is not a policy aspiration. It is the statutory architecture.

## 6. Cross-sector application: the "even the Home Office" argument

The following framing is available in any dispute with an institution relying on automated or blanket processing to avoid named human accountability. Full ready-to-send paragraphs are in the [template file](../../templates/GOVERNMENT_PARALLEL_ACCOUNTABILITY_WITH_BURGESS.md).

### 6A — Energy (unsigned warrants; E.ON Next)

The Home Office administers one of the most operationally complex support systems in government. It has converted named-caseworker individual review from a best-practice aspiration into a statutory requirement. An energy company seeking forced entry to a domestic property — one of the most serious exercises of private institutional power — cannot credibly argue that individual review is impractical.

Ask for: the named officer who reviewed the specific warrant application; the signed and dated warrant (Rights of Entry (Gas and Electricity Boards) Act 1954); the decision record.

*Anchor case:* E.ON Next unsigned warrant EG021819-26 — see [SOUL.md](../../SOUL.md).

### 6B — Local authorities (PCN / council tax enforcement; Darlington Borough Council)

The TPT allowed the appeal in DK00003-2605 (14 June 2026) because DBC could not establish the fundamental legal prerequisite — keepership — before enforcement. No named human had individually verified the specific fact on which the power depended. That is a structural NULL. The Home Office parallel makes clear this is not a technicality: it is the operative standard.

Ask for: the named officer who reviewed the specific case before the PCN was issued, the liability order was sought, or the enforcement agent was instructed.

*Anchor case:* TPT DK00003-2605 — see [LIVE_AUDIT_LOG.md](../../audits/LIVE_AUDIT_LOG.md).

### 6C — Medical devices and NHS audiology (Phonak / NHS)

A clinical device fitted or programmed for a specific patient without a named clinician reviewing that patient's specific audiogram, specific hearing profile, and specific expressed preferences is processing the patient as a member of a class — not as an individual. The Home Office parallel applies: where individual circumstances are relevant to treatment, a named professional must review those circumstances individually before institutional power is exercised.

Ask for: the named clinician who reviewed the specific audiological facts before the fitting decision; the clinical record; compliance with Medical Devices Regulations 2002 and Equality Act 2010 s.20.

*Anchor case:* MHRA FOI2026/00527; CEC 253215.

### 6D — Banks and financial services (automated credit; Equifax; Vanquis)

UK GDPR Articles 22A–22D (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) require that automated decision-making affecting individuals is subject to named human review on request. The Home Office reform reinforces that algorithmic or class-based processing is insufficient where individual circumstances are relevant to the outcome. A credit refusal, account restriction, or fraud classification based on automated scoring without individual named review is structurally NULL.

Ask for: the named officer who reviewed the specific account facts before the decision; confirmation of automated decision-making safeguards under Articles 22A–22D; the human review route.

### 6E — EASS / EHRC submissions

Parliament has legislated to require individual named-caseworker review of specific facts before conditional powers are exercised — precisely because blanket processing cannot satisfy the accountability requirements of modern administrative law. The Burgess Principle binary test provides the operational definition of "meaningful human involvement" that regulators require but institutions have rarely been compelled to measure.

### 6F — Parliamentary Standards Commissioner

The Home Office has enacted that blanket automated processing is insufficient when individual circumstances are relevant to entitlement. A Member of Parliament's office that responds to a constituent's correspondence without confirming that a named individual reviewed the specific facts of the specific situation is applying a structurally NULL process to a SOVEREIGN obligation.
