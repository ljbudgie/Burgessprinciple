## 1. What this document establishes

In March 2026, the Home Secretary restructured the UK's asylum support system from a blanket statutory duty into a conditional, evidence-based power — explicitly requiring named caseworker review of individual facts before support is withheld or withdrawn.

That is the Burgess Principle in statute.

Not as an external critique. Not as a campaigning framework. As enacted government policy.

This document maps the parallel precisely, provides the Hansard anchors, and translates the precedent into actionable language for use in disputes with energy companies, local councils, medical device providers, banks, AI systems, and any institution relying on automated blanket processing to avoid named human accountability.

---

## 2. The policy change: what the Home Secretary announced

On 5 March 2026, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced the Migration Reform package to Parliament. The central accountability change was the conversion of the support duty under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 from a near-automatic obligation into a conditional power, exercisable only where an individual satisfies specific conduct requirements verified through named caseworker review.

> **"We will remove the current legal obligation to provide asylum support… and restoring the power to offer support."**
>
> — Shabana Mahmood, Home Secretary, HC Deb 5 March 2026 (Migration Reform statement)

> **"The generosity of the British people will become conditional… On those seeking asylum following the law… Those who require it and play by the rules will rightly continue to receive asylum support. But those who do not will have their support removed."**
>
> — Home Secretary's speech, 5 March 2026

The legal mechanism: a Statutory Instrument under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999, converting Sections 4, 95, and 98 support from a **duty** (automatic, blanket, process-based) to a **power** (conditional, individual, evidence-based). Effective March / June 2026.

**The legal significance of this shift is precise:** a duty requires an institution to act on status alone. A power requires an institution to form a judgment about a specific individual based on specific facts. The first is architecturally NULL. The second, properly exercised, is SOVEREIGN.

## 3. The binary parallel — SOVEREIGN vs NULL, mapped to the reform

| Dimension | Blanket Duty (pre-reform) | Conditional Power (post-reform) |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis for decision** | Immigration status (automatic) | Named caseworker's review of individual conduct |
| **Evidence of specific facts** | Not required | Required (evidence of illegal working) |
| **Named decision-maker** | None — algorithmic / administrative | Named Home Office caseworker |
| **Audit trail** | System log | Individual decision record, open to judicial review |
| **Recourse if wrong** | Complaint (general) | Judicial review of named officer's decision |
| **Burgess classification** | **NULL** — no named human applied individual scrutiny | **SOVEREIGN** — named human applied specific-facts review before power was exercised |

The old system processed support by status. No named human needed to know anything about the specific person — the duty attached automatically to the classification. That is the definition of NULL: institutional power exercised without an individual mind applied to the individual case.

The new system requires a named caseworker to review specific evidence of specific conduct before the power to withhold is exercised. That is the definition of SOVEREIGN: a named human applied their mind to the specific facts of the specific case before institutional power was exercised.

**The Home Office did not invent this distinction. It recognised it — and enacted it in law.**

## 4. Why this validates the Burgess Principle's central argument

The Burgess Principle has argued since its registration (UK Certification Mark UK00004343685) that:

1. Blanket processing of individuals as a class, without individual review, is a structural deficiency — not merely an administrative inconvenience.
2. The remedy is not more oversight layers, complaint buttons, or policy reviews. It is a named human, with authority, reviewing the specific facts of the specific person before institutional power is exercised.
3. That standard is achievable. The same institutions that insist it is impractical already apply it in other contexts when the stakes are high enough.

The March 2026 Home Office reform is the government stating, in statutory form, that:

- Blanket automatic processing is insufficient when individual conduct is relevant to entitlement.
- A named human must apply their mind to the specific facts before the power is exercised.
- An audit trail of that individual decision must exist.

If that standard is appropriate for asylum support — one of the most operationally complex, high-volume areas of government administration — it is appropriate for an energy warrant, a PCN, a benefits sanction, a credit decision, and a medical device fitting.

The Home Office has not lowered the bar. It has confirmed the bar. Apply it here.

## 5. The Sections 4 / 95 / 98 mechanism — a legal note

For practitioners and lawyers:

**Section 95, Immigration and Asylum Act 1999** — the primary support provision for asylum seekers who are destitute. Pre-reform: near-automatic duty once eligibility criteria were met. Post-reform: discretionary power, conditioned on conduct compliance (including no evidence of illegal working).

**Section 4** — support for failed asylum seekers in prescribed circumstances. Same conversion from duty to power.

**Section 98** — temporary support while Section 95 eligibility is being determined. Same conversion from duty to power.

The Statutory Instrument effecting the change makes the conditionality explicit: the decision to withhold or withdraw must be preceded by a named caseworker's assessment of the individual's conduct against specific evidentiary criteria. The decision record must be capable of withstanding judicial review — meaning it must be individual, reasoned, and attributable to a named officer.

This is not a policy aspiration. It is the statutory architecture.

## 6. Cross-sector application: the "even the Home Office" argument

The following framing is available in any dispute with an institution that is relying on automated or blanket processing to avoid named human accountability.

### 6A — Energy (E.ON unsigned warrants; British Gas)

> The Home Office, administering one of the most complex and politically sensitive support systems in government, has converted its processing of individual cases from an automatic duty into a named-caseworker conditional power — precisely because blanket processing without individual review cannot satisfy the accountability requirements of modern administrative law.
>
> I ask that you apply the equivalent standard here. Specifically: identify by name and role the person who individually reviewed the specific facts of my case — including [insert specific issue: the warrant application / the debt recovery instruction / the billing decision] — before institutional power was exercised against me.
>
> A system log, a team reference, or a statement that your organisation uses human oversight does not satisfy this request. I am asking for the named individual who applied their mind to my specific facts.
>
> Relevant anchors: E.ON warrant EG021819-26; Rights of Entry (Gas and Electricity Boards) Act 1954 (warrant must be signed and dated by a named justice of the peace); Equality Act 2010 ss.20–21.

### 6B — Local government (Darlington Borough Council; PCN / council tax enforcement)

> The Home Office has explicitly enacted in statute that conditional powers affecting individuals require a named caseworker review of specific individual facts before the power is exercised. The same principle applies to parking enforcement, council tax liability, and enforcement agent instruction.
>
> Darlington Borough Council's enforcement of PCN [reference] was processed without individual human review of the specific facts of the specific vehicle's specific situation. The Traffic Penalty Tribunal (DK00003-2605, decision 14 June 2026) found accordingly that DBC had not established the fundamental legal prerequisite — keepership — before enforcement was pursued.
>
> I ask that you confirm: which named officer reviewed the specific facts of [this case] before [the PCN was issued / the liability order was obtained / the enforcement agent was instructed]?
>
> Relevant anchors: TPT decision DK00003-2605; LGO 26000967; Public Sector Equality Duty (Equality Act 2010 s.149).

### 6C — Medical devices (Phonak / Equality Act; NHS audiology)

> The Home Office conditionality reform demonstrates that where individual circumstances are relevant to entitlement or treatment, a named human must assess those individual circumstances before institutional power is exercised. The same applies to clinical device settings and NHS audiology provision.
>
> A clinical decision about a specific patient's hearing device settings, made without a named audiologist reviewing the specific audiogram, the specific hearing profile, and the specific patient's stated preferences, is structurally equivalent to the automated processing that the Home Office has now legislated against in its own domain.
>
> Please confirm: which named clinician reviewed the specific audiological facts of my specific case — including [the AutoSense OS settings / the fitting protocol / the device selection] — before the clinical decision was made and implemented?
>
> Relevant anchors: MHRA FOI2026/00527; Medical Devices Regulations 2002; Equality Act 2010 s.20; Phonak SAR.

### 6D — Banks, credit, and DSARs

> The Home Office has confirmed in statute that blanket processing of individuals by reference to a class or status — without a named human reviewing individual facts — is insufficient where individual circumstances are relevant to entitlement. This principle is directly applicable to automated credit decisions, account restriction, and fraud-classification systems.
>
> Please provide: the name and role of the person who individually reviewed my specific account facts — including [the transaction in question / the account restriction decision / the fraud classification] — before the institutional power was exercised against me. A reference to automated systems, risk models, or team-level oversight does not satisfy this request.
>
> Relevant anchors: UK GDPR Articles 22A–22D (DUAA 2025); FCA Consumer Duty (July 2023); ICO complaint [reference].
