# Email Triage as Automated Decision-Making — The Burgess Test Applied to Communications Infrastructure

**Part of:** The Burgess Principle · UK Certification Mark UK00004343685
**Status:** Documented NULL finding category — Communications Infrastructure
**Applies the binary test:** *Was a named human being's mind applied to the specific facts of this specific email before the decision was made about whether the recipient sees it?*

---

## 1. What email triage actually decides

Automated email triage systems — among them Google Priority Inbox, Microsoft
Copilot for Outlook, SaneBox, Superhuman, and Front — make decisions, on behalf
of an identified individual, about which correspondence reaches that person and
in what order. They sort, prioritise, defer, bucket, summarise, and in some
cases silently suppress incoming mail.

These are not trivial formatting choices. A decision about *whether and when a
person sees a message* is a decision with consequences. Correspondence buried or
deprioritised by triage may never be read at all. The effects reach into:

- **Legal rights** — deadlines, service of documents, responses to disputes;
- **Democratic participation** — correspondence between constituents and elected
  representatives;
- **Commercial relationships** — contracts, offers, disputes, time-limited
  notices;
- **Personal communications** — the ordinary human business of being reachable.

## 2. The binary test, applied

The Burgess Test asks one question of any system exercising power over an
identified person:

> **Was a named human being's mind applied to the specific facts of this
> specific email before the decision was made about whether the recipient sees
> it?**

In every automated triage system, the honest answer is **NULL**:

- no named human reviewer considered the specific message;
- no individual consideration of the specific facts took place;
- the decision was produced by an algorithm operating at bulk scale.

This is not an accusation of bad faith. It is an accurate description of the
architecture. Triage systems are *designed* to operate without a named human in
the loop — which is precisely why the result is structurally NULL rather than
incidentally so.

Two things follow, and they must be kept apart. That triage is *structurally*
NULL under the test is a description of its architecture, and it holds for every
automated triage system. Whether a given NULL carries **regulatory force** is a
separate, context-dependent question — answered not by the test alone but by the
significance of the effect in the specific case (§4). The test diagnoses; the
law attaches consequences only where the effect is significant.

## 3. The accountability gap

Communications infrastructure has never previously been named or tested as an
automated decision-making surface. Yet it sits upstream of almost every other
process the Burgess Principle examines: a person can only assert a right, answer
a complaint, or escalate a NULL finding if the correspondence reaches them in
the first place. An unaccountable triage layer is therefore a gap *beneath* the
other documented gaps.

## 4. Regulatory relevance

The strongest and most direct footing is **accessibility and correspondence
rights**:

- **Equality Act 2010 (ss.20–21)** — where email is a reasonable adjustment and
  the only accessible channel, triage that buries or mischaracterises that
  correspondence — or the volume it produces — engages the duty directly.
- **Article 8 ECHR** — the right to respect for private correspondence.
- **Article 10 ECHR** — freedom of expression, which includes the freedom to
  *impart* information to a recipient.

The position under **Articles 22A–22D UK GDPR** (as amended by the Data (Use and
Access) Act 2025, in force 5 February 2026) is narrower and more
context-dependent, and is stated here with that discipline. Article 22 governs
decisions *about an individual*, based solely on automated processing, that
produce legal or similarly significant effects — a deliberately high bar built
for decisions such as credit, benefits, and enforcement. Ordinary inbox ordering
will not usually meet it. Triage credibly engages Article 22 only in specific
contexts where the effect is significant — for example where it suppresses or
materially delays:

- **legal correspondence** (service, deadlines, disputes);
- **correspondence with an MP or public body** (democratic participation);
- **professional correspondence** with significant consequences.

Claimed indiscriminately, the Article 22 argument weakens. Claimed precisely — in
those contexts, and especially alongside an accessibility duty — it holds. The
framework is a precision instrument; applied to communications, that discipline
matters more, not less.

## 5. A live documented NULL finding chain

The risk is not hypothetical. The following chain is documented:

1. **Email triage (NULL).** Automated prioritisation determined how a UK
   constituent's correspondence to an MP's office was surfaced and perceived.
2. **Accessibility-blind volume characterisation (NULL).** The constituent has
   bilateral sensorineural hearing loss and relies on **email-only**
   communication as a reasonable adjustment under the Equality Act 2010
   (ss.20–21). Email was the *only accessible channel available*. The volume of
   correspondence was a product of that constraint — not of intent — yet the
   office characterised the correspondence as "persistent" without accounting
   for the disability that shaped its form.
3. **Police referral (NULL).** On the basis of that accessibility-blind
   characterisation, the office referred the constituent to the police.
4. **Downstream effect.** That referral contributed to a police attendance at a
   disabled constituent's home. The conduct recorded on body-worn video is now
   the subject of a Parliamentary Standards complaint.

At no point in this chain can a named human be identified as having applied
their mind to the specific facts — including the disability — before power was
exercised. Each link is a NULL finding, and the volume that triggered the chain
was itself shaped by triage and by the absence of an accessible alternative.

## 6. Why this matters

Naming email triage as an automated decision-making surface does not demand that
inboxes return to manual sorting. It asks something narrower and achievable:
that where automated triage produces significant effects, there is a route to a
named human who can review the specific facts — especially where accessibility
needs mean a channel is not optional but the only one available.

NULL here is the starting point and the diagnosis, not the verdict. SOVEREIGN —
a named human able to review the specific correspondence on request — remains the
destination.
