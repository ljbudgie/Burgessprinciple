# Eponymous Design — Why the Name Is Structural

The Burgess Principle is named after its originator. That choice is not branding.
It is a structural design decision that solves a recurring failure mode in open
standards for governance, policy, and accountability software.

This document records the rationale so that the design remains legible as the
project scales.

---

## 1. It resists semantic bleed

Generic terms dilute under institutional pressure. Phrases such as “ethical AI”,
“responsible automation”, and “meaningful human involvement” begin as precise
requirements and end as internal checkboxes. Institutions can claim compliance
with the *spirit* of a phrase while retaining the original process.

A proper noun does not dilute in the same way. An organisation cannot credibly
claim to follow “the spirit of the Burgess Principle” if it fails the binary
test published in this repository. The definition remains tethered to a single,
unambiguous question and to the three outcomes that follow from it:

- **SOVEREIGN** — a named human reviewed the specific facts of the specific case.
- **NULL** — no such review occurred.
- **AMBIGUOUS** — the response does not confirm individual review.

Either the test was applied as written, or it was not. The name makes that
boundary harder to blur.

---

## 2. It creates a dual boundary between free protocol and protected standard

The repository materials are released under the MIT licence. Anyone may fork,
adapt, translate, or build on the templates, code, papers, and methodology.

The name and the certification mark (UK00004343685) are governed separately.
An enterprise or contractor can fork the repository, alter the criteria, and
produce a different process. What it cannot do is label the altered process
“Burgess Principle compliant” or use the mark without authorisation.

This dual boundary keeps the protocol open while protecting the meaning of the
standard. The community repository remains the single source of truth for what
the test actually requires. See [LICENSE.md](./LICENSE.md) and
[CERTIFICATION_MARK.md](./CERTIFICATION_MARK.md).

---

## 3. It shifts the posture of the person raising the test

When an individual cites only generic legal language against a large institution,
the exchange often collapses into dense bureaucratic reply. A named principle
changes the register. Citing a specific, published test with a short handle
(“Burgess NULL”, “the Burgess binary test”) signals that the challenge rests on
an established framework rather than an isolated complaint.

This does not guarantee any particular institutional response. It does give the
person raising the test a clearer, more scannable instrument. English
administrative law has long used eponymous tests for precisely this reason:
short, unambiguous handles travel better than paragraphs of explanation.

---

## 4. It supports citability and doctrinal continuity

Once a short form exists in published papers and a public repository, later
filings, audits, and academic work can refer to it without re-deriving the
entire framework each time. The SSRN series and this repository establish
“Burgess NULL” and “Burgess SOVEREIGN” as precise taxonomic terms for the
presence or absence of individual human review.

That continuity is useful only if the definition stays stable. The eponymous
name, the certification mark, and the Core admission rule in [TIERS.md](./TIERS.md)
exist to keep the definition from drifting under external pressure or internal
scope creep.

---

## Practical implications for this repository

- Contributions that weaken, complicate, or rephrase the binary question itself
  are out of scope. See the Core admission rule in [TIERS.md](./TIERS.md).
- Use of the name or mark to imply official certification requires authorisation.
  Independent commercial use of the MIT materials does not.
- New findings, templates, and adaptations should strengthen the path from a
  decision to accountable human review, not create parallel or diluted standards.

For machine-readable citation of the project and its papers, see
[CITATION.cff](./CITATION.cff) and [papers/SSRN_INDEX.md](./papers/SSRN_INDEX.md).
