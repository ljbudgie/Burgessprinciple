# Insurance Underwriting / Claim / Renewal – Request for Human Review  
**— The Burgess Principle**

Use this when an insurer, underwriter, or claims handler has refused, delayed,
voided, non-renewed, re-priced, or restricted a policy or claim, and it is
unclear whether a named human reviewed *your* specific facts before acting.

Typical situations: automated quote refusal, claim triage decline, fraud-score
hold, medical underwriting without a named underwriter, motor/home/life/health
non-renewal, or a complaints outcome that only cites "our systems".

This is not regulated insurance advice. It is a calm request for confirmation
of individual human scrutiny. For free guidance on insurance complaints, see
the Financial Ombudsman Service and MoneyHelper.

---

**Date:** [DATE]  
**Reference:** [Policy / Claim / Quote / Case Reference]  
**To:** [Insurer / Underwriting / Claims / Complaints Team]

Dear [Team],

I hope this finds you well.

I am writing about [briefly describe the decision in one sentence, e.g. "the
refusal of my home insurance claim [claim number]", "the decline of my motor
quote", "the non-renewal of my policy", "the loading / exclusion applied at
underwriting", or "the fraud investigation hold on my claim"].

Following the guidance in the Burgess Principle, I would like to kindly ask:

**Was a human member of your team able to personally review the specific facts of *my* individual situation before this insurance decision was made?**

If someone did review my case, I would be grateful for a short written
confirmation including:

1. The name and role of the reviewer (or an internal reference that identifies them).  
2. The date of that review.  
3. Which of my specific facts were considered (for example the circumstances of
   the loss, medical or risk disclosures, prior claims explanations, disability
   or vulnerability markers, or corrections I already supplied).  
4. Confirmation that the review took place **before** the decision was issued
   to me.

If that did not happen, I would appreciate my case now receiving that personal
human attention, rather than remaining the product of automated underwriting,
claims triage, fraud scoring, or standard policy rules alone.

For clarity, a general statement that your systems include human oversight,
that claims are "reviewed in line with policy", that underwriting is
FCA-compliant or Consumer Duty aligned, or that complex cases are escalated,
does **not** confirm that a human member of your team personally reviewed the
specific facts of my case. Please provide a direct **YES** or **NO** answer to
the Burgess Principle question above. If **YES**, please also identify the
reviewer by name and role.

So that I can understand and respond, please also confirm:

1. What exact decision was made and when it first took effect.  
2. Whether the decision relied on automated underwriting, pricing models,
   claims triage tools, fraud analytics, third-party data, or medical coding.  
3. Whether any human claims handler or underwriter assessed my explanation and
   supporting evidence before the decision remained in place.  
4. What further information, if any, you need from me for a fair individual
   review.  
5. How I can escalate under your complaints process (FCA DISP) if needed,
   including the date from which any final-response clock runs.

Optional details I can provide on request:

- **Policy / claim type:** [home / motor / life / health / travel / other]  
- **Facts the automated path may have missed:** [e.g. correct cause of loss,
  temporary change of use, disability-related needs, timing of disclosure,
  repair estimates already sent]  
- **Vulnerability / accessibility needs:** [if any]

I am keen to cooperate. I simply ask that my situation be treated as an
individual insurance matter requiring real human judgment.

Thank you for your time.

I am also requesting reasonable adjustments under the Equality Act 2010
(sections 20 and 29) if applicable. My preferred method of communication is
email only.

Yours sincerely,  
[Your Full Name]  
[Your Contact Details]  
[Policy / Claim reference]
