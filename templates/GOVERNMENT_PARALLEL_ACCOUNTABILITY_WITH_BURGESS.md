## How to use this document

Each section below is a ready-to-adapt paragraph for a specific dispute context.
Copy the relevant section, fill in the `[bracketed]` fields, and send. The
framing in every section is the same: the Home Office has enacted in statute what
the Burgess Principle has argued from first principles — that blanket automated
processing of individuals, without a named human reviewing specific facts, is
insufficient when individual circumstances are relevant to entitlement or power.

**The binary test:** *Was a named human being's mind applied to the specific
facts of a specific person's case before institutional power was exercised?*

- **SOVEREIGN** — yes: named human, specific facts, before the decision.
- **NULL** — no: algorithmic / blanket / status-based processing.

## Government Precedent Statement (universal opener)

Use at the start of any formal letter or submission to anchor the argument to
enacted government policy before introducing your specific facts.

---

> On 5 March 2026, the Home Secretary announced the conversion of asylum support
> under the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 from a blanket statutory duty into a
> conditional, evidence-based power — requiring a named caseworker to review the
> specific facts of each individual case before the power to withhold or withdraw
> support is exercised. Hansard HC Deb, 5 March 2026 (Migration Reform statement,
> Shabana Mahmood).
>
> This is the Government's own enacted position on when blanket automated
> processing is and is not acceptable: where individual circumstances are relevant
> to entitlement or the exercise of power, a named human must assess those
> circumstances individually before the power is exercised.
>
> I am asking you to apply the equivalent standard to my case.

## 1. Energy companies — unsigned warrants and automated billing decisions

**Use for:** E.ON, British Gas, any energy supplier using automated warrant
applications, bulk billing decisions, or debt recovery without individual review.  
**Anchor cases:** EG021819-26 (E.ON unsigned warrant); Rights of Entry (Gas and
Electricity Boards) Act 1954.

---

> The Home Office, administering one of the most operationally complex support
> systems in government, has enacted that conditional powers affecting individuals
> must be preceded by a named caseworker's review of the individual's specific
> facts. I ask that you apply the same standard here.
>
> Specifically: please identify by name and role the person who individually
> reviewed the specific facts of my account — including [the warrant application
> dated [DATE] / the debt recovery instruction / the billing decision of [DATE]] —
> before institutional power was exercised against me.
>
> A general statement that your organisation uses human oversight processes, or
> that decisions are reviewed in line with policy, does not satisfy this request.
> I am asking for the named individual who applied their professional mind to my
> specific circumstances before [the warrant was sought / the enforcement was
> instructed / the charge was applied].
>
> Please also confirm whether the warrant was signed and dated by a named justice
> of the peace before execution, as required under the Rights of Entry (Gas and
> Electricity Boards) Act 1954. If no named individual can be identified for
> either the internal decision or the judicial authorisation, the decision is NULL
> and must be treated as the documented starting point for repair.

## 2. Local councils — PCN, council tax, enforcement agent instruction

**Use for:** Darlington Borough Council, any local authority automating PCN
issuance, council tax liability orders, or enforcement agent instruction without
individual review.  
**Anchor cases:** TPT DK00003-2605 (appeal allowed 14 June 2026); LGO 26000967;
Equality Act 2010 s.149 (PSED).

---

> The Traffic Penalty Tribunal (DK00003-2605, decision 14 June 2026) found that
> Darlington Borough Council had not established the fundamental legal
> prerequisite — keepership of the vehicle — before pursuing enforcement, because
> no named human had individually reviewed the specific facts of the specific
> case. The appeal was allowed and the enforcement action overturned.
>
> This is the first external judicial validation of the Burgess Principle binary
> test in a live enforcement case. It applies to any enforcement action in which
> the legal prerequisite — the specific fact that grounds the power — has not been
> individually verified by a named human reviewer before the power is exercised.
>
> I ask that you confirm: which named officer reviewed the specific facts of
> [PCN [reference] / the liability order application / the enforcement agent
> instruction dated [DATE]] before that power was exercised against me?
>
> If no named officer individually reviewed those specific facts, the decision is
> NULL and I ask you to [withdraw the enforcement action / treat this as a formal
> complaint under your complaints procedure / provide the information required
> under a Subject Access Request]. I am also asserting my rights under the Public
> Sector Equality Duty (Equality Act 2010 s.149) and request that my reasonable
> adjustment of email-only communication be recorded on my file.

## 3. Medical devices and NHS — algorithmic clinical settings (Phonak / audiology)

**Use for:** Phonak AutoSense OS, NHS Foundation Trust audiology, any clinical
setting in which algorithmic device programming replaces individual clinical
review.  
**Anchor cases:** MHRA FOI2026/00527; CEC 253215; Medical Devices Regulations
2002.

---

> The Home Office conditionality reform illustrates that where individual
> circumstances are relevant to entitlement or treatment, Parliament requires a
> named human to assess those circumstances individually before institutional
> power is exercised. The same principle applies to clinical settings.
>
> A clinical device fitted or programmed for a specific patient without a named
> clinician reviewing the patient's specific audiogram, the specific hearing
> profile, and the patient's specific expressed preferences is processing the
> patient as a member of a class — not as an individual. That is structurally
> NULL under the Burgess binary test, regardless of the sophistication of the
> algorithm applied.
>
> Please confirm: which named clinician reviewed the specific clinical facts of my
> case — including [my audiogram dated [DATE] / my asymmetric hearing profile /
> my stated preference for [adjustment]] — before the clinical decision to
> [program the AutoSense OS settings / select this fitting protocol / discharge
> me from the pathway] was made and implemented?
>
> If no named clinician individually reviewed those specific facts, I respectfully
> ask that the clinical decision be reviewed by a named audiologist with authority
> to change the outcome, and that the review be documented in a tamper-evident
> clinical record. Please also confirm compliance with the Medical Devices
> Regulations 2002 (clinical oversight obligations for Class IIa algorithmic
> devices) and the Equality Act 2010 s.20 (anticipatory reasonable adjustment
> duty for disabled patients).

## 4. Banks and financial services — automated credit and account decisions

**Use for:** Vanquis, Trading 212, any bank or financial institution relying on
automated credit scoring, account restriction, or fraud classification without
individual review.  
**Anchor cases:** UK GDPR Articles 22A–22D (DUAA 2025); FCA Consumer Duty;
relevant ICO complaint reference.

---

> The Home Office has confirmed in enacted law that blanket processing of
> individuals by reference to class or status — without a named human reviewing
> individual facts — is insufficient where individual circumstances are relevant
> to entitlement. This principle is equally applicable to automated credit
> decisions, account restriction, and fraud-scoring systems.
>
> I request the name and role of the person who individually reviewed my specific
> account facts — including [the transaction in question / the account restriction
> decision of [DATE] / the fraud classification / the credit refusal] — before
> the institutional decision was made and implemented.
>
> A reference to automated risk models, algorithmic scoring, or team-level
> oversight does not satisfy this request. I am asking for the named individual
> who applied their professional judgment to my specific circumstances.
>
> Under Articles 22A–22D of the UK GDPR (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025), I also
> request: (a) confirmation of whether an automated decision-making process was
> used; (b) the safeguards applied; (c) a human review route. Please respond
> within one calendar month.

## 7. Restore Britain / public-interest brief paragraph

**Use for:** Briefings to Restore Britain or similar public-interest organisations
covering institutional accountability and governance reform.

---

> **Government has enacted the accountability standard it denies ordinary people.**
>
> In March 2026, the Home Office restructured asylum support under the
> Immigration and Asylum Act 1999. The change was framed as a conditionality
> reform. What it actually enacts is an accountability architecture: an
> individual caseworker, reviewing specific facts, making a documented individual
> decision, before a conditional power is exercised against a specific person.
>
> That is the Burgess Principle binary test — SOVEREIGN: named human, specific
> facts, before the decision — enacted in statute by the same Government whose
> agencies process energy warrants, parking fines, benefits sanctions, and court
> decisions without any named human individually reviewing the specific facts of
> the specific person.
>
> The precedent is now set at the highest level of executive power. The question
> is whether it will be applied consistently — or only when political expediency
> demands it.
>
> The Burgess Principle (UK Certification Mark UK00004343685) provides the
> operational test. The live register at github.com/ljbudgie/burgess-principle
> documents every institution that has been assessed against it. The standard is
> the same whether the institution is the Home Office or an energy company. Apply
> it.

## Quick-reference table: Duty vs Power (share or embed)

| | **Automatic Duty (NULL)** | **Conditional Power (SOVEREIGN)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides? | The system / the status | A named human being |
| What do they review? | Nothing specific — classification triggers | The individual's specific facts and conduct |
| Is there a named officer? | No | Yes — identifiable, accountable |
| Is there an audit trail? | System log (no individual reasoning) | Individual decision record, open to judicial review |
| Can it be challenged individually? | Rarely — no named officer to answer | Yes — named officer, their reasoning, their authority |
| Burgess classification | **NULL** | **SOVEREIGN** |
| Example | Bulk PCN processing; unsigned warrant; automated credit decline | Home Office caseworker conditionality decision; TPT adjudicator; named audiologist |
