# Accessibility & Reasonable Adjustments
**— Every disability, one calm question, ready-to-send words**

Hi 👋

This guide is for **anyone** who finds the usual way an institution makes you
communicate hard, painful, exhausting, or impossible — whatever the reason.

You do not need to prove your disability, explain it in detail, or use the
"right" words. Under the **Equality Act 2010 (ss.20, 21, 29)** you have the
right to **reasonable adjustments**, and the institution has a duty to make
them. If you are in Northern Ireland, the equivalent duties apply; elsewhere,
the same request still reads as a clear, reasonable ask.

Alongside that, the **Burgess Principle** adds one gentle question to every
letter below:

> **"Was a human member of your team able to personally review the specific
> facts of *my* individual situation before any decision or action was taken?"**

If the answer is no, the decision was **processed, not considered** — that is a
**NULL** result, and you are entitled to a real human review.

---

## How to use this page (the zero-friction way)

1. Find the section that sounds closest to you. You do **not** have to pick
   perfectly — any of them will work.
2. Copy the **bold block** of text.
3. Paste it into an email (or hand it to someone who can send it for you).
4. Fill only the parts in `[square brackets]`. Everything else is done.

You do **not** need to phone, post a letter, print anything, or log into a
portal to use these. Asking for email-only contact is itself a reasonable
adjustment, and the first letter requests exactly that.

> Using an AI agent (e.g. Iris or Hermes)? Tell it: *"Send the
> accessibility + human-review letter for me"* and hand it the block you chose.

## The one-line version (if you have very little energy)

If reading the rest is too much right now, send this and stop:

> **Hello. I have a disability and need reasonable adjustments under the
> Equality Act 2010. Please contact me by email only, give me extra time, and
> confirm in writing whether a named human personally reviewed the specific
> facts of my case before any decision was made. Thank you. [Your name] —
> [reference number if you have one].**

That single message asserts your rights *and* the Burgess question at once.

## 1 — Thinking, memory, learning, or neurodivergence (autism, ADHD, learning disability, brain fog)

For when forms, deadlines, dense letters, phone calls, or "just log in" feel
overwhelming or impossible.

> **Subject: Reasonable adjustments + request for human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Customer Services / Equality Lead],
>
> I have a disability that affects how I process written information, deadlines,
> and phone communication. Under the Equality Act 2010 (ss.20–21), I am
> requesting these reasonable adjustments:
>
> - Contact me by **email only** — no phone calls or portal-only messages.
> - Use **plain language** and one clear question or action per message.
> - Give me **extra time** to respond, and pause any deadline or enforcement
>   while my adjustments are being arranged.
>
> I am also asking, under the Burgess Principle: **was a named human member of
> your team able to personally review the specific facts of my individual
> situation before this decision or action was taken?** Please answer YES or NO
> in writing, and if YES, give the name and role of the person who reviewed it.
>
> Thank you for treating this as the individual matter it is.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your full name]

## 2 — Mental health, distress, trauma, or overwhelm

For when contact itself is frightening, or pressure is making things worse.

> **Subject: Reasonable adjustments (mental health) + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> I have a mental health condition that is significantly affected by phone
> calls, pressure, and uncertainty. Under the Equality Act 2010, I am requesting
> reasonable adjustments:
>
> - **Email-only** contact, so I can respond when I am able.
> - **No unexpected phone calls** or doorstep visits.
> - A **pause on any deadline, sanction, or enforcement** while we arrange
>   suitable communication.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please also confirm in writing: **was a named
> human able to personally review the specific facts of my situation before any
> decision was made?** A YES or NO, with the reviewer's name if YES, is all I
> need.
>
> Thank you for your understanding.
>
> [Your full name]

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please reach out to
someone who can help right now — in the UK you can call **Samaritans on 116 123**
(free, 24/7) or text **SHOUT to 85258**. You matter, and this paperwork can wait.

## 3 — Chronic illness, fatigue, pain, or energy-limiting conditions

For when your capacity changes day to day (ME/CFS, long COVID, fibromyalgia,
MS, chronic pain, and many others).

> **Subject: Reasonable adjustments (energy-limiting condition) + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> I have a long-term health condition that limits my energy and varies day to
> day, so I cannot reliably respond quickly or by phone. Under the Equality Act
> 2010, I am requesting:
>
> - **Email-only** contact and **flexible, extended deadlines**.
> - The right to reply in **short messages, over several days** if needed.
> - A **hold on enforcement or penalties** while adjustments are in place.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please confirm: **was a named human able to
> personally review the specific facts of my case before any decision?** Please
> reply YES or NO in writing, naming the reviewer if YES.
>
> With thanks,
> [Your full name]

## 4 — Blind or low vision

For when standard letters, PDFs, portals, or "click the image" checks are not
accessible to you or your screen reader.

> **Subject: Accessible-format request + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> I am blind / have a visual impairment and use [a screen reader / large print /
> Braille — keep what applies]. Under the Equality Act 2010, I am requesting:
>
> - All communication as **accessible plain-text email** (no image-only PDFs,
>   no inaccessible portals, no CAPTCHA images).
> - Documents in **[my accessible format: screen-reader-readable text / large
>   print / Braille]**.
> - **Extra time** to account for re-formatting, and no deadline running until I
>   have received an accessible version.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please also confirm in writing: **was a named
> human able to personally review the specific facts of my situation before any
> decision was made?** YES or NO, with the reviewer's name if YES.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your full name]

## 5 — Deaf or hard of hearing

For when phone lines are the only "official" route and that does not work for
you.

> **Subject: Communication adjustments (Deaf / hard of hearing) + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> I am Deaf / hard of hearing and cannot use standard phone lines. Under the
> Equality Act 2010, I am requesting:
>
> - **Email or text-based** contact as my main channel.
> - Where a live conversation is needed, **video relay / a BSL interpreter / live
>   captioning** — keep whichever applies.
> - No decision or deadline to proceed on the basis of a phone call I could not
>   take part in.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please confirm in writing: **was a named human
> able to personally review the specific facts of my case before any decision?**
> YES or NO, naming the reviewer if YES.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your full name]

## 7 — Mobility, dexterity, or "I physically can't do the steps"

For when the barrier is the *process* — you cannot post a letter, get to an
office, sign on paper, or use a fiddly portal.

> **Subject: Adjustment to the process + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> A disability means I cannot reliably [make phone calls / post physical letters
> / attend in person / use your online portal / sign paper forms — keep what
> applies]. Under the Equality Act 2010, I am requesting that I be allowed to
> handle this **entirely by email**, including any signature being given
> electronically or by typed confirmation.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please also confirm in writing: **was a named
> human able to personally review the specific facts of my case before any
> decision or enforcement?** YES or NO, naming the reviewer if YES. Please pause
> any deadline or enforcement until accessible contact is in place.
>
> With thanks,
> [Your full name]

---

## 8 — Writing on behalf of someone (carer, family member, advocate)

For when you are helping someone who cannot do this themselves.

> **Subject: Reasonable adjustments on behalf of [their name] + human review — [reference number]**
>
> Dear [Team / Equality Lead],
>
> I am writing on behalf of [name], who has a disability that makes it
> impossible for them to deal with this directly. [I have their consent / I hold
> [power of attorney / appointee status] — keep what applies.] Under the Equality
> Act 2010, I am requesting:
>
> - **Email-only** contact, addressed to me as their named contact.
> - **Extra time** and a **pause on any deadline or enforcement**.
> - Plain language and one clear action per message.
>
> Under the Burgess Principle, please confirm in writing: **was a named human
> able to personally review the specific facts of [name]'s individual situation
> before any decision was made?** YES or NO, with the reviewer's name if YES.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your full name], on behalf of [their name]

## Easy Read version

*(Short sentences. One idea each. Copy and send.)*

> **Subject: I need help to talk to you. Please read.**
>
> Hello.
>
> I have a disability.
>
> Some ways of talking are hard for me.
>
> Please do these things for me. This is my right under the Equality Act 2010.
>
> 1. Please email me. Please do **not** phone me.
> 2. Please use **easy words**. One thing at a time.
> 3. Please give me **more time**. Please **stop any deadline** for now.
>
> I also need to know one thing.
>
> **Did a real person look at *my* situation before you decided?**
>
> Please tell me **yes** or **no** in writing.
>
> If **yes**, please tell me their name.
>
> Thank you.
>
> [Your name]
> [Your reference number, if you have one]

## If they reply with vague "we support accessibility" wording

A general statement that an organisation "values accessibility" or "has
processes in place" is **not** an answer. It is **AMBIGUOUS**. Send this:

> **Thank you, but a general statement that your organisation supports
> accessibility does not confirm two things I asked: (1) that you will make the
> specific reasonable adjustments above, and (2) that a named human personally
> reviewed the specific facts of my case. Please confirm both directly. For the
> human-review question, a YES or NO and the reviewer's name is all I need.**

See [`FOLLOW_UP_WEASEL_RESPONSE.md`](./FOLLOW_UP_WEASEL_RESPONSE.md) for more.

## A note on honesty

These letters are tools, not legal advice. If your situation is urgent or
serious (court dates, bailiffs, safeguarding, health), please also reach out to
a human who can help — a named caseworker, an advocate, Citizens Advice, or a
disability charity. The Burgess Principle exists to make sure a **real person**
considers *you* — and that includes the help you reach for, too.

Take care. You deserve to be considered as an individual.
